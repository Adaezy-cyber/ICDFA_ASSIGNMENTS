# Evidence Bundle 04 — AUP Communication and Training Plan

## 1. Objective

The objective is to ensure that NexusTech users understand the new Acceptable Use Policy, know what is expected of them, can identify prohibited behaviour, and know how to report suspected security issues.

## 2. Communication and Training Matrix

| Audience | Key Message | Channel | Owner | Timing | Acknowledgement | Success Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All employees | What changed, why the AUP matters, acceptable/prohibited use, reporting responsibilities. | Company-wide email + intranet/policy repository | Information Security | Day 1 | Policy acknowledgement | ≥95% acknowledgement by Day 7 |
| All employees | Practical examples of acceptable use, personal use, remote work, information sharing, and incident reporting. | Short live/recorded training | Information Security | Days 2–5 | Training attendance/completion | ≥90% completion by Day 7 |
| IT / Helpdesk | Reporting, escalation, access, monitoring, and support responsibilities. | Role-specific briefing | Information Security + IT Lead | Days 2–4 | Attendance | 100% of relevant staff briefed |
| Managers | Manager responsibilities, access decisions, policy reinforcement, and escalation. | Manager briefing | Information Security + HR | Days 3–5 | Acknowledgement | ≥95% completion |
| HR | Onboarding/offboarding, acknowledgement records, and personnel escalation. | HR process briefing | Information Security + HR | Days 2–4 | Process acknowledgement | HR workflow updated |
| Contractors | Scope of AUP, prohibited use, information protection, and reporting. | Contract onboarding + email | Procurement/HR + Information Security | Before/at access | Attestation | 100% of in-scope contractors acknowledge |

## 3. Rollout Timeline

### Day 1 — Announcement

- Publish the approved AUP.
- Send organisation-wide announcement.
- Explain why the policy was introduced and what changed.
- Provide access to the policy and training.

### Days 2–5 — Training

- Deliver short training sessions.
- Use practical scenarios rather than policy text alone.
- Provide role-specific guidance for IT, managers, HR, and contractors.

### Day 7 — Initial Measurement

- Review acknowledgement and training completion.
- Identify teams or individuals with outstanding actions.
- Collect common questions and areas of misunderstanding.

### Day 14 — Follow-Up

- Send targeted reminders.
- Address repeated misunderstandings.
- Escalate persistent non-compliance through the appropriate management/HR process.

## 4. Acknowledgement / Attestation

Users should acknowledge that they:

1. have received access to the AUP;
2. understand their responsibilities;
3. know how to report suspected security incidents or policy violations; and
4. understand that violations may be subject to investigation and appropriate organisational action.

## 5. Effectiveness Measures

The rollout should be evaluated using measurable indicators, including:

- percentage of in-scope users who acknowledge the AUP;
- percentage completing required training;
- number and type of repeated AUP-related questions or misunderstandings;
- policy-related incidents or violations after rollout;
- time taken to resolve outstanding acknowledgements.

## 6. Escalation

Where a user does not complete required training or acknowledgement, the responsible manager should follow up.

Repeated misunderstanding should result in targeted refresher training or clarification.

Repeated or deliberate non-compliance should be escalated to the appropriate manager, Information Security, HR, Compliance, or Legal function depending on the nature and severity of the issue.

The objective of escalation is both **risk reduction and accountability**, not simply completion of a training checkbox.
