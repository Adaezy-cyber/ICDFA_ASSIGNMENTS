# Evidence Bundle 6: Segregation of Duties and Assurance

## 1. Weakness Register

| ID | Weakness / Conflict | Risk Created | Roles | Recommended Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOD-01 | IT Director owns security, risk approval and operations. | Concentrated authority and weak challenge. | IT/Security/Risk | Separate CISO governance, IT execution and CRO/Risk challenge. |
| SOD-02 | Technical implementer can influence acceptance of its own risk. | Self-approval. | IT/CISO/CRO/Risk | Independent challenge and authorised risk-owner acceptance. |
| SOD-03 | BU leaders make local security choices independently. | Inconsistent controls across offices. | BU/IT/CISO | Enterprise requirements plus governed local implementation. |
| SOD-04 | No formal cross-functional challenge forum. | One-function decisions. | IT/CISO/Business/Legal/Finance/HR | Security Governance Committee with defined authority. |
| SOD-05 | CEO/Board have limited visibility. | Material risks may not receive timely attention. | CEO/Board/CISO/CRO | Standard executive/Board reporting and thresholds. |
| SOD-06 | Policy decisions may be driven by implementation teams. | Governance requirements may become operationally convenient. | CISO/IT/Business | CISO-led policy governance with executive approval. |
| SOD-07 | Incident response and executive escalation can be confused. | Delayed material escalation. | IT/CISO/CEO | Separate response execution from escalation authority. |

## 2. Proportionate SoD

The objective is not to create an approver for every operational action. Separation should focus on decisions where conflict is material:

- implementation vs risk acceptance;
- security assessment vs enterprise-risk challenge;
- business demand vs security exception;
- incident response vs materiality decision;
- technical evidence vs legal/regulatory interpretation.

## 3. Decision Recording and Assurance

Every significant decision should have a unique ID linked to the relevant risk, incident, project, exception or policy record.

The record should identify:
- decision authority;
- evidence considered;
- alternatives;
- rationale;
- action owner;
- due date;
- residual risk;
- review date;
- closure evidence.

### Assurance checks

| Assurance question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Was the correct person accountable? | RACI & decision log |
| Was risk acceptance authorised? | Risk record & approval |
| Was independent challenge provided? | CRO review or  minutes |
| Was the decision implemented? | Action or technical evidence |
| Was residual risk reviewed? | Updated risk assessment |
| Was material risk escalated on time? | Escalation record |
| Were overdue actions followed up? | Action register |

## 4. Governance Effectiveness Review

At least annually, TechGlobal should review:
1. whether the structure remains appropriate;
2. whether committees make decisions rather than merely exchange information;
3. whether RACI assignments remain accurate;
4. whether material risks reach the correct authority;
5. whether risk acceptance receives independent challenge;
6. whether Board reporting supports decisions;
7. whether recurring conflicts indicate unclear authority.

A governance issue is closed only when the authorised decision is made, required treatment is completed or formally accepted, evidence is attached, residual risk is recorded, and the owner confirms completion.
