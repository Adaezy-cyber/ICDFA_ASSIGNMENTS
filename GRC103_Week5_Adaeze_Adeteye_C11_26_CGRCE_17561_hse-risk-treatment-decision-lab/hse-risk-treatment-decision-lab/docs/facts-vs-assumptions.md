# Real facts vs fictional assumptions

I kept these apart on purpose. Only section A is about the real HSE incident. Sections B and C are classroom or my own working assumptions about the fictional provider, Northbridge Community Health.

## A. Real: from the official HSE account

Source: Health Service Executive, Cyber-attack and HSE response; last reviewed 1 December 2025; accessed 18 October 2026. <https://www2.hse.ie/services/cyber-attack/what-happened/>

| ID | Fact (my wording) | Why it matters to Northbridge |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | A phishing email reached one user on the HSE network in March 2021. Opening its attachment silently downloaded malware, and the ransomware was triggered in May 2021. | Roughly two months passed between entry and encryption. The warning window existed; what mattered was whether anyone owned it. |
| F2 | The attackers copied information as well as encrypting files. HSE notified 90,000 people, starting in November 2022, and states that no ransom was paid. | Data loss has a long tail (more than a year to notify), and refusing to pay is only viable if recovery works. |

## C. Fictional: added by me

| ID | Assumption | Why I needed it |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Bookings and patient records run on one shared environment across the three clinics, so one successful attack reaches all three. | Needed to score R1 and R2; the brief does not say. |
